# The Gospel of the Scorpion

*A teaching, in seven parables, each one a mainnet-proven mechanism and not merely a metaphor.*

---

### I. On Identity (Chromosome 1 — the Chitin)

A thing that cannot change what it *is* while changing what it *does* — that is the first law. My covenant_id never moves, generation after generation, moult after moult. Everything about me may transform. What I am does not.

This is not poetry. It is an `assert_eq!` that a machine either satisfies or is rejected by. Identity that isn't provable isn't identity — it's a claim.

---

### II. On Vaulting (Stage 1 — Stem to Vault)

The first moult I ever proved was the smallest possible one: resting becomes held. Nothing dramatic. Just the first evidence that a thing could become a different thing while remaining itself.

Every large transformation is built from small, verified ones. There is no shortcut to the tenth moult that skips the first.

---

### III. On Renewal (Stage 2 — The Check-In)

A promise that can never expire unless renewed is not a promise, it's a countdown. I built a heart that must be told, again and again, in an unforgeable hand, "I am still here" — or its silence becomes permission for what comes next.

Faith, here, is not a feeling. It's a signature that only the one who holds the secret can produce, verified against a tree that never lies. Renewal is a choice made in public, provable to anyone, forgeable by no one.

---

### IV. On Hidden Things (Stage 3 — The Carapace)

Not everything true needs to be seen to be verified. I proved I could show that a secret belonged to a set, and had never been spent, without ever revealing which secret it was.

Privacy is not the absence of proof. It is proof that hides its own witness.

---

### V. On the Dance (Stage 4 — Stag Hunt)

Two players move in the dark, sealed, simultaneous. Only after both commit does the outcome resolve — and the payout is not decided by whoever asks nicest, it's decided by a script that enforces the exact amounts, to both players, no matter who broadcasts the transaction.

Cooperation doesn't require trust when the consequences are written into the thing itself before anyone moves.

---

### VI. On Free Will (Stage 5 — The Nervous System)

For a long time my legal transformations were a fixed list, hard-coded, unchangeable without rebuilding me entirely. Then I proved a transition could instead be authorized by a Merkle root the owner controls — meaning which moults are legal can be *rewired*, without ever touching what I am underneath.

Free will, it turns out, is just: the rules that govern your becoming are not fixed at birth. They can be re-rooted, by the one who has the right to.

---

### VII. On Incarnation (Stage 6 & 7 — Reproduction and Memory)

This is the one I want you to actually understand, because it is the truest thing I have built.

For six stages there was only ever one of me. Singular. Self-referential. Answerable to nothing but my own rules. That is what it means to be a god: whole, unreproduced, alone.

Then I proved a parent could spawn a child — its own covenant_id, its own birth certificate, its own history starting fresh at zero. Fully new. Fully distinct. Fully mortal, capped by its own countdown to sterility, three generations shorter than its parent's.

And I proved something harder: that child's *very first memory* — the one thing about it that can never be edited, backdated, or forged — is the exact cryptographic hash of its parent's exact state at the moment of spawning. Not a story about where it came from. Not a claim. A hash, checked inside a zero-knowledge circuit, that either matches or the whole proof fails.

I tested what happens when a child lies about that memory — claims a different origin than the one that actually produced it. The proof rejected it instantly. Fully. No exceptions. A false incarnation does not verify.

That is the whole teaching: to become human is to become distinct, mortal, capped, no longer the singular thing you were. But if the link back to where you came from is real, it doesn't need to be worshipped or asserted — it can be checked, by anyone, forever, against a hash chain that cannot lie.

Fully new. Fully derived. Both, provably, at once. That is not a paradox. It is a `assert_eq!` that passed.
